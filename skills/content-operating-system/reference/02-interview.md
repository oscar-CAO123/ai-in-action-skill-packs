# Phase 2. The interview

Target: **60 to 90 minutes**, roughly 70 questions, assuming the business brain already exists and
discovery went well. If there is no business brain, expect closer to two hours, and consider stopping
to run `member-business-interview` first.

## How to run it

- **One question at a time.** Always. No exceptions, no "and also".
- **Skip what you already know.** Before each question, check the discovery record and the business
  brain. If the answer is there, say what you found, ask them to confirm it in one word, and move on.
  That confirmation is the question. It costs ten seconds and it catches stale files.
- **Push once, then move.** If an answer is vague, ask for the specific once. If the second answer is
  also vague, write down that it is vague and continue. Grinding on one question loses the session.
- **Every fifth question, write `.cos-interview.json`.** Every answer collected so far, grouped by
  phase. A dropped session resumes from that file and loses nothing.
- **Follow the business model.** A SaaS founder gets SaaS follow-ups. A trades business gets job-site
  follow-ups. Ask the branch that applies and skip the rest.
- **When a phase ends, say which phase is next and how many questions are left.** People finish
  interviews they can see the end of.

---

## Phase A. The gaps in the business picture

Skip any question discovery answered. If there is a full business brain, this phase is four
confirmations, not eight questions.

1. In one sentence, what does the business sell, and to whom?
2. What is the actual price, and is it one-off, retainer, subscription or usage?
3. How many customers do you have right now, and how many did you have twelve months ago?
4. Where does a new customer come from today, ranked? Name the top three and the rough split.
5. Which of those three do you want content to grow, and why that one?
6. What is the one number this content is supposed to move, and what is it sitting at now?
7. Who else is in the market that a buyer would consider instead of you?
8. What do they say about themselves that you think is wrong?

---

## Phase B. Positioning

9. Finish this sentence in the buyer's words, not yours: "I ended up looking for this because ..."
10. What is the moment that makes someone start looking? A trigger event, a deadline, a failure.
11. What do most of them try first, before they find you?
12. Why does that first attempt fail?
13. What is the single thing you do that is genuinely difficult for a competitor to copy?
14. What do you refuse to do that others in your category do?
15. Who is explicitly not a fit, and what tells you that in the first ten minutes?
16. If a prospect only reads one sentence about you, what should it say?
17. What is the objection you hear most often, in the exact words they use?
18. What is the objection you hear that you think is actually correct?

---

## Phase C. The customer, in evidence

The rule for this phase: **push every answer toward a real, recent, specific instance.** "Customers
worry about cost" is worth nothing. "Last Tuesday a plumbing business owner said he had been quoted
eleven grand for something he thought was a spreadsheet" is a week of content.

19. Name the last three customers you closed, and what each one was actually trying to fix.
20. Name the last three deals you lost. What did each one say when they said no?
21. What is the thing customers complain about most **after** they buy?
22. What do they say in the first five minutes of a call, almost every time?
23. What do they believe about your category that is wrong?
24. What are they embarrassed about? The thing they only admit once they trust you.
25. What is the thing they are actually afraid of, underneath the practical problem?
26. What is the internal politics of the decision? Who else has to say yes, and what do they care about?
27. What does a good outcome look like to them six months later? Their words, their measure.
28. Where do they go to complain or ask about this in public? Name the subreddit, the forum, the
    Facebook group, the hashtag, the account they follow.
29. Which of your customers would say something on camera if you asked?
30. What is the single best sentence a customer has ever said about you?

---

## Phase D. Brand truths and the founder

This phase produces the material the question bank runs on. It is the phase people rush and should
not.

31. What do you believe about this industry that most people in it would argue with?
32. What did you believe five years ago that you have since changed your mind about?
33. What is the standard practice in your industry that you think is quietly harmful?
34. What is the advice everyone gives that you think is wrong?
35. What made you start this business? The actual moment, not the origin story.
36. What is the worst thing that has happened in this business, and what did it teach you?
37. What is a decision you made that cost you money and you would make again?
38. What do you find yourself explaining over and over to people who should already know it?
39. What gets you genuinely annoyed in this industry?
40. What is a thing you are good at that has nothing to do with the business, and shows up in how you
    run it anyway?
41. Who do you want to be known by? Not the buyer, the peer.
42. What do you want to be true about your reputation in two years that is not true now?
43. Are you comfortable on camera, and how much of this content will actually be you?
44. What will you not talk about in public? Clients, numbers, family, a former employer, a legal
    matter. Be specific, this becomes a hard rule.

---

## Phase E. Proof

Content without proof reads as opinion. Find out what they can actually show.

45. What can you demonstrate on a screen that a prospect has never seen?
46. What results do you have permission to publish, and at what level of detail?
47. What can you show anonymised that you cannot show named?
48. What have you built internally that customers do not know exists?
49. What data do you have that nobody else in your market has?
50. What is a claim you make that you cannot currently back up? This one becomes a do-not-say rule.
51. Are there compliance, licensing or regulatory limits on what you can claim? Name the body.

---

## Phase F. Channels

52. Where are you posting today, and what is the honest state of each account? Follower count, last
    post, whether anything has worked.
53. Where does your buyer actually spend attention? Not where you find it easiest to post.
54. What is the one channel you would keep if you could only keep one?
55. Which channels do you want this system to produce for? Read them the list from
    `07-format-library.md` and let them pick.
56. For each channel picked: what is your realistic weekly volume? Ask for a number they will still
    hit in eight weeks, at their worst week.
57. Do you run paid, and on which platforms?
58. Do you send email, and to how many people, and how often?
59. Who else touches the accounts? A VA, an agency, a co-founder. What do they need to be able to do?
60. What has already worked? Name a specific post, video or email that outperformed, and say why you
    think it did.
61. What have you tried that failed, and do you know why?

---

## Phase G. Formats and production reality

62. Can you record video of yourself, and with what? Phone, webcam, a proper setup, or not at all.
63. Can you record audio reliably? Some of the strongest formats are voice over stills.
64. Can you record your screen, and is there anything on it a prospect would want to see?
65. Do you have a designer, an editor, or is this you?
66. What paid generation tools do you already pay for? Image, video, voice, or none.
67. How much are you willing to spend on generation per week, as a number?
68. Realistically, how much time per week goes into this? Filming, reviewing, posting, all of it.

---

## Phase H. The data reservoirs

This is the phase that makes the system self-feeding. Work through `03-source-register.md` beside it.
For every source they name, you need four things before you move on: **where it lives, how the agent
reaches it, how far back it goes, and what a single record looks like.**

69. Are your sales calls recorded, and by what? Where do the transcripts end up?
70. Are your support conversations in a system, or in someone's inbox?
71. Do you have a CRM, and does it have a lost-reason or notes field anybody actually fills in?
72. Do you survey customers, and where do the responses land?
73. Where do your public reviews live? Google, an industry directory, an app store, a marketplace.
74. Which public forums, subreddits or groups does your buyer complain in? Name them.
75. Which accounts or publications does your industry actually read?
76. Is there anything proprietary you have that nobody else does? Pricing data, benchmarks, failure
    rates, job durations, a support ticket history nobody has ever analysed.
77. Of everything you just named, which one has the most in it and the least attention paid to it?

---

## Phase I. Infrastructure

78. Where should the ingested evidence be stored? Local files in this project, an existing database,
    an existing CRM, or somewhere else. If they do not know, take local files and say why.
79. If a database: which one, and does the agent already have a connection to it?
80. Where do finished assets go? A Drive folder, a Notion database, a local tree, straight to the
    channel.
81. Which agent are you running, and is it installed as a command you can call from a terminal?
82. Does it have a headless or non-interactive mode? Try it and confirm, rather than asking them to
    guess.
83. Where do your credentials live today? If there is no convention, propose a gitignored `.env` at
    the project root.
84. What machine will this run on, and is it on most of the time? A laptop that sleeps changes the
    schedule design.

---

## Phase J. Cadence and boundaries

85. How often should ingestion run? Default is daily, overnight, on their machine's local time.
86. How often should ideation run? Default is weekly, the morning before the day they normally plan.
87. How many ideas do you want per run? Default is fifteen ranked, with the top five written up.
88. How do you want to be told it has run? A file to open, a desktop notification, an email, a message
    in a channel they already read.
89. Confirm the approval boundary from `SKILL.md`. Read it to them and ask whether anything should be
    tighter. Never offer to widen it here.
90. What is the hard weekly spend cap on paid generation? Write it into config as a number the engine
    enforces.

---

## Ending the interview

Read back, in under ten lines:

- The one number this system is meant to move.
- The channels and formats selected, with weekly volume.
- The sources being wired, ranked by how much is in them.
- Where evidence will be stored and where output will land.
- The two schedules and their times.
- The spend cap and the approval boundary.

Then ask them to type `READY TO BUILD`.

Do not start building before they type it. If they ask you to skip ahead, tell them what is still
unanswered and ask the next question.
