# The interview

60 to 90 minutes, one sitting or four. One question at a time, and the answer is written down
before the next one is asked. Answers land in `.machine-build.json`.

If `content-operating-system` was run before this, read its `.cos-interview.json` first and ask
only what it does not already answer.

Two rules for whoever is asking:

- **Ask for the sentence, not the summary.** "What did they say?" beats "what was their concern?"
  Verbatims are what make the brain worth having.
- **Write down what they do not know.** An unanswered question is a gap in the brain, and a gap
  recorded honestly is worth more than a plausible guess.

---

## Block 1. The business

1. What do you sell, in the words you would use to a stranger at a barbecue?
2. What does it cost, and how is that priced?
3. What happens in the first thirty days after somebody buys?
4. What do you do that a competitor genuinely cannot?
5. Who else are they choosing between, including doing nothing?
6. What is the thing people misunderstand about what you do?

## Block 2. The offers

For each offer, one at a time:

7. What is the promise, in one sentence?
8. What proof do you have that it works? Name it: a result, a case, a number, a named client.
9. What is the objection that comes up every single time?
10. Who is it for, and who is it explicitly not for?
11. Is there a free thing that leads into it, and what does that free thing give away?

**An offer with no proof is recorded with no proof.** That decides what content can honestly claim
about it, and it is the single most useful thing this block produces.

## Block 3. The avatars

12. Who actually buys? Give them a job title and a situation, not an age bracket.
13. What does their week look like on the day they finally go looking for you?
14. What have they already tried, and why did it not work?
15. What are they afraid will happen if they get this decision wrong?
16. Who else is in the room when they decide?
17. What do they call the problem, in their words?

Each avatar gets a short code here: `CLINIC`, `TUTOR`, `FLORIST`. Use whatever names these people
by their situation in your business.

## Block 4. The pains

18. What are the five things customers most often say is wrong, before they buy?
19. For each, what is the sentence they actually say?
20. Are there figures? Hours a week, dollars a month, a percentage. Whatever they quoted.
21. What is the cost of leaving it alone for another year?
22. Which of these do you have proof you fix?

Each pain gets a slug and its home angle: the framing this business puts over that cluster.

## Block 5. The channels and the references

23. Where do you publish now, and where do you wish you published?
24. What is measured on each one, and what number do you actually look at?
25. Which pieces of your own have worked? Name them, and say why you think so.
26. Whose content do you want this machine reading? Accounts, channels, subreddits, newsletters,
    repositories, publications.
27. For each: are you reading them for the shape of their content, for their angles, or for both?
28. Whose content should it never imitate?

## Block 6. The reservoirs

29. Are sales or discovery calls recorded? Where do the recordings or transcripts land?
30. Is that recording consented, and under which rules for where you operate?
31. Where do customer reviews and testimonials live?
32. Where do support conversations live?
33. What is in the CRM that says why a deal was won or lost?
34. Is there a document somewhere that already says most of this?

**Nothing is read from any reservoir in this block.** The block establishes what exists and whether
it may be read. Phase 4 does the wiring, after the director has seen the list.
