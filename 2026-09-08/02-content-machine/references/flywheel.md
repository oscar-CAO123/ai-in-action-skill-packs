# The seven stages

The cadence the machine runs on, weekly. Stages 1 to 3 are scheduled and run without anybody
present. Stages 4 to 7 are a person's, with the agent doing the work they ask for.

Every stage stamps gaps rather than failing. A missing credential, a quiet channel, a week with no
calls: recorded on the run, printed, carried into the summary, never fatal. A quiet week still has
to produce.

---

## Stage 1. The internal read

**What is already working here, on one ledger.** Organic and paid together: what works organically
works better with spend behind it, so the two sit on the same page rather than in two reports.

Three inputs:

| Input | Where it comes from | What it gives |
|---|---|---|
| the channel read | each channel's API, or a hand export when there is no API | which pieces won, in the window |
| the hand rank | a person ranking a piece on the board | the winners the director names, stored as a snapshot marked `manual` |
| the call read | the week's new sales, discovery and support call transcripts | the pains, the words and the figures buyers actually said |

**Two windows, ending yesterday: 7 days and 28 days.** A piece can win the week and lose the
month, and both facts are worth having.

**Ranking is inside the channel.** Rank every piece that channel reported, on that channel's own
metric, so rank 1 means best on the channel. Comparing a view against a click across channels
produces a number that means nothing.

**What a winner is read on.** Each winner is decomposed on the three variables of the formula, so
the next stage can see which part carried it: the avatar it was written to, the pain it was built
on, and the packaging (the format, and inside it the hook structure, the script structure and the
visual components).

**One guardrail, from paid.** Never turn a piece off while its ad set is hitting its target.
Last-click makes the top-of-funnel piece that set up the sale look like the one to kill.

---

## Stage 2. The external read

**What is working for others in this space.** The reference profiles and sources named in the
interview, scored by engagement rather than by taste, across three targets:

1. **Direct competitors**, the businesses selling something similar.
2. **The accounts this audience already watches**, whoever they are.
3. **Shapes winning everywhere**, regardless of subject.

The external read feeds format, angle and structure. **The internal read outranks it on avatar and
pain**, because a business's own calls say what its buyers have, and the outside says how the rest
of the world is packaging it.

**A reference supplies structure, never language.** The scan returns other people's words by
design. Read them for the shape, then write the line from `brain/hooks.md`.

---

## Stage 3. Ideation

Both reads become the week's concepts as formulas. No hook is written, no script is drafted,
nothing is generated. The stage codifies what to make and lands it for review.

**The formula is `avatar x pain x format`**, each carrying a code, so the whole set is searchable
on any of the three:

| Variable | The code | From |
|---|---|---|
| avatar | the avatar code, or `general` | `brain/avatars.md` |
| pain | the pain theme slug, which carries its angle | `brain/pains.md` |
| packaging | the format id | `brain/formats.md` |

**Pain and angle are one thing.** The angle is the business's framing over a cluster of pains, and
each theme has one home angle, so naming the theme names the angle.

**Which variable to hold.** When a piece won, one of its variables carried it: hold that one, vary
the others, and write on the idea which is which. When the evidence is flat, hold the pain first
and the avatar second, and never hold the packaging, because packaging is the cheapest thing to
change and the least likely to have been the cause.

**The mix.** Roughly sixty percent extends what has proven itself, forty percent is new ground.
Both numbers move with how much proof exists. With no proof at all the run is a cold start: a small
fixed set of new formulas, written down as new, so nobody later mistakes a guess for a finding.

**The gate.** Every formula is validated before it lands: known avatar, known pain, known format,
a code that resolves, no empty fields, and no batch where one avatar takes every slot. A formula
that fails is logged with the reason and dropped.

**What lands.** One batch, its formulas as `ideas` rows, each carrying the evidence that produced
it: which winners it drew from, what it holds, what it varies.

---

## Stage 4. Authoring

**A person approves a formula, and approval produces its skeleton**: the hook as a cited structure
with its slots, the script as its named beats, the shot list as a beat count, the packaging outline,
and the awareness stage. Structures, never filled lines.

The director then fills it, in any order, with the agent doing the writing they ask for. Hooks
first is the recommended move, because a hook captures the concept in the fewest words and a
rejected hook takes its script with it.

**Nothing waits on the rest of the batch.** One piece being unfinished never blocks another.

**Every filled part clears the copy gate** from `brain/laws.md` before it moves on. A finalised part
is never regenerated: the machine does not overwrite the human.

---

## Stage 5. Production

The authored batch becomes a render queue and the engine takes it, one rig per format.

**Free formats first, one paid generation at a time.** Any paid run on an unproven format stops and
shows the director before a second one is bought. The gates sit before the spend, never after it.

---

## Stage 6. Post-production

Human, always. The piece is refined to the director's taste, gets its cover, and clears the batch
gate. The passing gate result travels with the batch, so "done" means something a machine checked
and a person looked at.

---

## Stage 7. Publishing

Scheduled, then published by a person. **The post URL is pasted back onto the row at publish**, and
that single act is what closes the loop: it is how stage 1 finds this piece on the platform next
week and writes its performance back onto the same row.

A machine that publishes on its own is a different decision, with different risks, and this pipeline
does not make it.

---

## What the cadence protects against

- **Building on a hunch after the numbers arrived.** Once the read exists, the read decides, and
  the forty percent is a deliberate allocation rather than a default.
- **The batch that is one idea restated.** The gate caps avatar concentration and demands distinct
  pains.
- **Producing against a formula nobody approved.** Every formula is gated before a word is written.
- **Producing against unfinished copy.** Every part exists before anything is generated, because a
  render against unfinished copy is paid for twice.
- **The machine overwriting the person.** A finalised part is never regenerated.
